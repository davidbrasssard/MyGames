How I chose (assumptions to review):
- Only one picture per kind of subject; where two were close I kept one. Dropped for being near-twins: rose (tulip), banana (looks like the crescent moon), strawberry (apple), dog (cat), ladybug (butterfly), a second tree (evergreen), pink flowers (hibiscus, cherry blossom, lotus). Dropped for low contrast on ivory: rabbit (replaced by cow). Hyacinth was dropped because its file was 83 KB even after optimizing; replaced by the camera. So flowers are only two (sunflower, tulip).
- Full-body animals only; no animal faces (cat face, panda, koala, frog...) to stay clear of the "no faces" rule. The owl has a face by nature; I kept it because it is instantly recognizable.
- "Orange" is Fluent's "Tangerine" picture; "Livre" is the blue book; "Soleil et nuage" is Fluent's "sun behind cloud" (the plain sun looks too much like the sunflower).
- Colors: many pictures are red/pink/green, but no two share both color and shape. Look hardest at: tulip / parrot / car / mushroom (all pink-red), and elephant / cow / swan (grey-white). These are the ones I would swap first.
- Names: FR/EN names are my wording ("Raisin" singular for grapes, "Maïs", "Appareil photo", "Plante en pot").
- Each picture is an <img> pointing at a file; the optimizer shortens internal ids (a, b, c...), which is fine for <img> but means these SVGs must NOT be pasted inline into the page together (the ids would clash).
- Some pictures use SVG blur/shadow effects inside the file (that is how Fluent draws its soft edges). They are drawn once per image, not animated, so this does not break the "no heavy effects" rule, but watch performance of a full board on the Galaxy Tab 4.
- The setting "collection" (default "nature") is a different thing: it is for backgrounds/photos later. "tiles-fluent" is the Mahjong tile set.
- The preview screen text is plain French and not in the FR/EN dictionary because it is development-only.
